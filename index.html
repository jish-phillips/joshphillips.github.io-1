About

I’m a lecturer in the Department of Linguistics at Stanford University. My research primarily investigates the semantics and pragmatics of time and possibility expressions—intensional categories such as tense, modality, aspect, and negation. I’m particularly interested in how the linguistic encoding of these concepts varies across language communities and evolves over time.

A core pillar of my research program explores linguistic variation and universals within the "irreality" domain—the mechanisms human languages utilize to describe hypothetical and counterfactual situations. My current book project centers on providing an intensive account of the structure, development, and variation of the verbal paradigm in Yolŋu Matha, a language family spoken in Northern Australia.
